## Revision Notice (v1.0 → v2.0)

Version 1.0 of this document designated FieldEffectCrc **Cohort A** as the
discovery cohort. Phase 1's mandatory batch-effect audit (the exact
methodological safeguard this project was designed around, per the lesson
from P2.1) found that Cohort A cannot be used this way. This section
documents what was found and why the architecture changed, since this
pivot is itself a meaningful part of the project's methodology, not just
a housekeeping note.

**What the audit found:** PCA and UMAP on Cohort A's top 2000 variable
genes showed sub-study explaining more variance than biology (PCA: 0.707
vs. 0.374; UMAP: 0.823 vs. 0.433). A crosstab of study origin against
sample type showed why: every "healthy" (HLT) sample in Cohort A comes
from exactly two sources (BarcUVa, GTEx), and every tumor (CRC) sample
comes from entirely different sources (predominantly TCGA, plus four
smaller clinical sites). Biology and data source are almost perfectly
confounded for the tumor-vs-healthy contrast — a classifier trained on
this task would be unable to distinguish real tumor biology from which
lab processed the sample. This is a more severe version of the exact
problem that invalidated P2.1's first candidate dataset. Batch correction
cannot fix this: when a class exists in only one or two batches, batch and
class are not statistically separable, correction would either erase real
signal or bake the confound into the model.

**What changed as a result:**
- The discovery cohort changed from FieldEffectCrc Cohort A to **TCGA-COAD**.
- The primary endpoint changed from **tumor vs. healthy** to **tumor vs.
  tumor-adjacent normal (NAT)** — a cleaner, less confounded contrast, and
  one that both TCGA-COAD and several validation cohorts share a common
  definition for (see Decision 4 in the original log, which anticipated
  exactly this distinction).
- FieldEffectCrc Cohort A's healthy-tissue-only sources (BarcUVa, GTEx) are
  dropped entirely. Its four non-TCGA clinical sites (HebeiMU, KoreaAMC,
  KoreaPNU, Mayo) are repurposed as a new external validation set, since
  they contain both CRC and NAT samples and are not confounded the way the
  full cohort was.
- FieldEffectCrc Cohort B was checked for patient overlap against the new
  discovery cohort before being finalized as a validation set (see Decision
  7 below) — this overlap check is exactly the kind of due diligence this
  project's methodology is built to require before trusting any dataset
  pairing.

These figures (the PCA/UMAP plots and the crosstab) are being kept as a
dedicated methods figure in the final report, not discarded, since catching
this before any modeling code was written is itself evidence the project's
audit protocol works as intended.

## A. Executive Recommendation

Build a **leakage-safe, repeated-nested-cross-validated, multi-cohort external-validation study** of colorectal tumor-vs-tumor-adjacent-normal classification, where the actual research contribution is **not** the classification itself (biologically easy, already done many times in the literature) but a **systematic comparison of three feature-selection strategies** (differential-expression-informed, data-driven, pathway-level) on **which one yields features that remain predictive, stable, and biologically interpretable across four independent, technically and biologically distinct cohorts.**

This design directly demonstrates the methodological maturity gained from P2.1 (cohort confounding awareness), and now also from P2.2's own Phase 1 (catching and correctly resolving a near-identical confound before any modeling code was written), while asking a scientifically distinct question. It produces a portfolio piece that is defensible to a skeptical reviewer, not just a "high accuracy" showcase.

## C. Literature Positioning

Tumor-vs-normal CRC classification from bulk RNA-seq is **well-established** — not a novel task. Representative prior work:
- A 2023 study (TCGA-CRC + GSE50760 discovery, LASSO + 5 ML models, external validation on GSE142279) reported a 3-gene signature (CA2, CA7, ITM2C) reaching ~99–100% AUC on external data — illustrating both that near-perfect separation is common for this task, and that such numbers alone are not a meaningful research contribution.
- Numerous other CRC prognostic/diagnostic signature papers validate on 1–5 external GEO cohorts but rarely treat cross-cohort **feature stability** as a first-class question, and rarely enforce patient-level, leakage-safe nested CV as a design principle from the outset.
- On the methods side, peer-reviewed tooling exists specifically for our exact problem (e.g., the `nestedcv` R package, built for embedded feature selection in high-dimensional transcriptomic data with proper nested CV), and there is a well-documented literature on the optimistic bias introduced when feature selection precedes CV splitting.

**Our legitimate contribution:** not "ML can classify CRC" (solved), but a reproducible, leakage-safe comparison of feature-selection strategies for cross-cohort robustness and biological interpretability, evaluated across four cohorts that differ deliberately in protocol, lab, and normal-tissue definition — explicitly designed to detect the kind of cohort confounding that undermined P2.1's first dataset.

## D. Recommended Study Design

```
TCGA-COAD (discovery)
   │
   ▼
Outer CV (patient-level grouping; 5-fold × 5 repeats, stratified)
   │
   ├── Inner CV: feature selection (3 strategies, done independently per fold)
   │        A) DE-informed (limma-voom or simpler filter, trained-fold only)
   │        B) Data-driven (variance/univariate filter + stability selection)
   │        C) Pathway-level (MSigDB Hallmark, mean standardized z-scores)
   │
   ├── Inner CV: hyperparameter tuning (Elastic Net / RF / XGBoost)
   │
   └── Outer fold evaluation (ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, balanced accuracy, etc. — averaged across the 5 repeats)
   │
   ▼
Freeze final model (fit on ALL of TCGA-COAD, using the winning representation)
   │
   ▼
Apply frozen model, no refitting, to:
   Val. 1 (GSE196006+GSE251845) → Val. 2 (FieldEffectCrc clinical sites) →
   Val. 3 (FieldEffectCrc Cohort C) → Val. 4 (FieldEffectCrc Cohort B, TCGA-free)
   │
   ▼
Feature stability analysis (bootstrap resampling within TCGA-COAD)
   │
   ▼
Biological interpretation (enrichment, cell-composition caveat check)
   │
   ▼
Exploratory-only: score the leftover FieldEffectCrc healthy-tissue samples
(BarcUVa/GTEx) with the frozen model, purely as a field-effect probe —
never used for training or feature selection, clearly labeled exploratory
```

## E. Research Questions

**Primary RQ:**
Which transcriptomic feature-selection strategy — differential-expression-informed, data-driven, or pathway-level — produces colorectal tumor-vs-tumor-adjacent-normal classification features that remain predictive across four independent cohorts differing in sequencing protocol, tissue-normal definition, and originating lab?

**Secondary RQs:**
1. How much does classification performance degrade from internal (repeated nested CV) to external validation, and does the degradation pattern differ by feature-selection strategy?
2. Which genes are selected repeatedly (high-confidence, stable) across bootstrap resamples and across models, and are they enriched for biologically coherent pathways after correcting for background bias?
3. Does a classifier trained on tumor vs. tumor-adjacent normal assign genuinely healthy tissue (the FieldEffectCrc BarcUVa/GTEx samples excluded from discovery for confounding reasons) a score even further from tumor than NAT — consistent with a field effect gradient of healthy → NAT → tumor? This is explicitly exploratory: those samples are confounded with data source, so any result here is hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory.
4. Do candidate stable features show associations with likely tissue/cellular composition (immune, stromal, epithelial marker genes), such that "predictive" must be qualified as "associated with tissue composition" rather than presumed tumor-cell-intrinsic biology?

---

## F. Hypotheses

- **H1:** Internal (repeated nested CV) AUC will be high (>0.90) for all three feature-selection strategies, given the strong tumor/normal biological effect size, though with wider confidence intervals than a v1.0-style analysis would have shown, owing to TCGA-COAD's thin normal class.
- **H2:** External performance will drop most for FieldEffectCrc Cohort C (single-end, cross-protocol) relative to the paired-end validation sets, providing a direct, quantified demonstration of technical-confound sensitivity.
- **H3:** The data-driven and DE-informed strategies will select overlapping but not identical gene sets; the pathway-level representation will show smaller performance drops across cohorts due to its coarser, more robust aggregation, at the cost of interpretability at the single-gene level.
- **H4:** A consensus set of features selected across ≥2 strategies and stable under bootstrap resampling will be enriched for well-established CRC-associated processes (cell cycle, DNA replication/repair, extracellular matrix remodeling) — consistent with, but independently derived from, the DEG signal already observed in the earlier CDDL/GSE251845 exploratory work.
- **H5 (exploratory):** The frozen model will assign the excluded FieldEffectCrc healthy-tissue samples scores further from "tumor" than it assigns tumor-adjacent normal samples, consistent with a field effect — evaluated as exploratory and hypothesis-generating only, given these samples' known confounding with data source.

## G. Final Pipeline (raw/processed data → biological interpretation)

1. **Data acquisition & harmonization** — download all five cohort sources; verify Ensembl gene ID / genome build compatibility; record accession IDs, download dates, versions. *(Complete for all four datasets as of Phase 1.)*
2. **QC & mini-audit** — PCA/UMAP of the discovery cohort colored by any available source/site metadata; confirm no dominant internal batch structure before proceeding. *(Complete — this step is what caught the original Cohort A confound and drove the v2.0 pivot. TCGA-COAD does not have the same multi-source pooling structure Cohort A did, since it's a single harmonized GDC pipeline, but a lighter version of this check — e.g. by tumor stage or submission batch if available — is still worth running before Phase 2 begins.)*
3. **Preprocessing** — establish a normalization strategy defensible for cross-cohort application (e.g., within-cohort log-CPM + gene-wise standardization fit on training data only; explicit decision on whether/how to attempt any batch adjustment, with ComBat use only if independently justified per Section 16 of the original brief).
4. **Patient-level repeated nested CV on TCGA-COAD** — outer 5-fold × 5 repeats (grouped by patient), inner CV for feature selection + hyperparameter tuning, for each of the 3 feature-selection strategies × 3 models (Elastic Net, Random Forest, XGBoost).
5. **Model freezing** — fit final chosen configuration(s) on all of TCGA-COAD.
6. **External validation** — apply frozen model, unmodified, to Validation 1–4 in sequence; report ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, sensitivity/specificity, balanced accuracy, with bootstrap CIs where feasible.
7. **Feature stability analysis** — bootstrap resampling within TCGA-COAD; build consensus feature set using pre-specified selection-frequency thresholds.
8. **Interpretation** — SHAP (tree models) / coefficients (Elastic Net) for global feature importance; explicit "SHAP describes contribution to predictions, not causation" caveat.
9. **Biological enrichment** — GO/KEGG/Reactome/Hallmark on the consensus feature set, background = genes passing the expression filter (not whole genome), BH-FDR correction.
10. **Tissue-composition check** — compare consensus features against curated immune/stromal/epithelial marker gene sets; frame any overlap as "associated with tissue composition," not tumor-cell-intrinsic discovery.
11. **Secondary/exploratory field-effect analysis** — score the excluded FieldEffectCrc healthy-tissue samples with the frozen model; visualize healthy→NAT→CRC ordering if supported by the data, with the confounding caveat stated explicitly wherever this result appears.
12. **Write-up** — Introduction, Dataset & audit (including the Cohort A pivot as a methods figure), Methods, Results (internal CV, external validation, stability, enrichment, secondary analyses), Discussion, Limitations, Conclusion.

## H. Methodological Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Hidden batch structure within the discovery cohort | Cohort A's mini-audit already caught and resolved one such case (Decision 7). A lighter equivalent check on TCGA-COAD (e.g. by submission batch/site if metadata allows) is still worth running before Phase 2 |
| Cross-cohort normalization inadvertently removing real biological signal, or leaking information | Fit all normalization/scaling parameters on training data only; no joint normalization across discovery + validation cohorts |
| Overselling near-ceiling accuracy as novel | Explicit framing throughout: classification itself is not the contribution; robustness/interpretability is |
| Treating "normal" as one biological category across cohorts | Resolved more thoroughly than in v1.0 — every cohort's "normal" is now NAT specifically, not a mix of healthy and adjacent tissue |
| Thin normal class in the discovery cohort (~41 samples) causing unstable feature selection or optimistic performance estimates | Repeated stratified nested CV (5×5) rather than single-pass 5-fold (Decision 9); wide confidence intervals reported rather than point estimates alone |
| Small validation cohorts (Cohort B: n=22; clinical sites: n=38) giving noisy external estimates | Report bootstrap confidence intervals; avoid overinterpreting point estimates from small cohorts |
| TCGA-COAD's severe class imbalance biasing interpretation | Mandatory use of PR-AUC/balanced accuracy/sensitivity-specificity; accuracy never reported alone |
| Multiple external cohorts inviting post-hoc "cherry-picking" of the best-performing one | Pre-specify that all four validation results are reported together, regardless of outcome |
| Gene ID / annotation version mismatches across cohorts silently shrinking usable feature overlap | Explicit gene-overlap audit step, already run against real data: 34,677 genes confirmed usable across all five roles |
| Validation 2 and Validation 4 sharing four underlying clinical institutions (different patients) | Disclosed explicitly as a limitation rather than presented as fully independent evidence |
| Repeating the exact confound-detection failure that this whole pivot was built to catch, somewhere further downstream | The audit script and its synthetic-data validation are documented and reusable — the same check should be re-run on any future dataset considered for this project, not treated as a one-time fix |

## Addendum (v2.1) — Decision 11: Cohort C TCGA-origin leakage found and corrected

**Context.** During Phase 4 external validation, Cohort C (the deliberate
cross-protocol stress test) showed unexpectedly strong performance
(ROC-AUC 0.81–0.91), in direct contradiction of the pre-registered
expectation (Hypothesis H2) that it would underperform relative to the
same-protocol validation cohorts. Rather than accept a result that
contradicted its own pre-specification, the discrepancy was investigated
before being written up as a finding.

**What was found.** Cohort C's study composition (`study` field) showed
195 of its 207 tumor samples (94%) originate from TCGA — the same source
as the TCGA-COAD discovery cohort — while its normal-class samples come
almost entirely from a different, unrelated source (MtSinai, 47 of 56).
A direct patient-ID overlap check (mirroring the Cohort B check from
Decision 10) found that **135 of these 195 TCGA-origin patients (69%)
are literally the same patients as in the TCGA-COAD discovery cohort.**
This is not a confound — it is data leakage. Cohort C's strong Phase 4
result was substantially inflated by the model having effectively
already seen a majority of its tumor-class patients during training.

**The fix.** Consistent with the Decision 10 precedent for Cohort B, all
195 TCGA-origin samples were removed from Cohort C entirely — not just
the 135 confirmed overlaps — for one clean, simple rule applied
uniformly across the whole project: zero TCGA-origin samples anywhere
outside the discovery cohort. This leaves a genuinely independent
Cohort C of 80 samples (CityOfHope, MtSinai, Singapore, TexasAM, Utah,
ZhejiangU), 68 of which fall within the primary tumor-vs-NAT endpoint
after excluding 12 healthy-tissue samples (12 CRC, 56 NAT).

**Corrected result.** Re-running Phase 4 on the cleaned Cohort C dropped
its ROC-AUC from 0.81–0.91 down to 0.46–0.74 across the 9 frozen
pipelines — confirming the original result was leakage-driven, not a
sign of genuine cross-protocol robustness.

**Updated Phase 4 summary across all four leakage-free validation cohorts:**

| Cohort | N (primary endpoint) | Gene coverage | ROC-AUC range | Read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Validation 1 (GEO pooled) | 86 | 99.8% | 0.94–0.99 | Strong, credible generalization |
| Validation 2 (clinical sites) | 38 | 57.2% | 0.47–0.60 | Near chance |
| Validation 3 (Cohort C, cleaned) | 68 | 57.2% | 0.46–0.74 | Modest, mixed |
| Validation 4 (Cohort B, cleaned) | 22 | 57.2% | 0.50–0.73 | Modest, mixed, small-N |

The sharp divide tracks gene coverage far more than protocol per se:
strong, consistent generalization only holds on the cohort with
near-complete gene overlap with the discovery cohort (Validation 1);
every FieldEffectCrc-derived cohort, sharing only ~57% gene overlap due
to a different annotation pipeline, shows substantially weaker and more
volatile performance regardless of sequencing protocol. No single
feature-selection strategy dominates across the three harder cohorts.

**Why this is being kept as a visible finding, not a quiet correction.**
This is the second confound this project's own methodology has caught
mid-analysis (after the Cohort A pivot in the original Revision Notice),
and both are being retained in the final report as dedicated methods
findings. A pipeline that only ever produces clean, expected results
without ever having to correct itself is not more trustworthy than one
that catches and documents its own mistakes — it is less scrutinized.
